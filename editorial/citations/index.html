    3.15 Citations & References¶

    Last reviewed by the SAP DTP team: 2026-05-17.

    How to cite books, articles, websites, scriptures, and other sources in SAP English publications. SAP follows the Chicago Manual of Style (CMS), Notes–Bibliography (NB) system — the convention used in history, religion, and other humanities disciplines — with BAPS-specific departures for Hindu shastra citations (see 3.15.7).

    For the placement of footnote superscripts in running text (after a period, before a comma, etc.), see 3.11 Footnotes. This page covers the content and structure of each citation; 3.11 covers where the superscript goes.

    Show me — citations in a paragraph

    The Vachanamrut records that Bhagwan Swaminarayan delivered 273 discourses between V.S. 1875 and V.S. 1886 (Vachanamrut Gadhada I 1).¹ Bhagwan Krishna’s teaching on disciplined action without attachment to results is foundational here (Bhagavad Gita 2.47). For an overview of the early development of the tradition in English-language scholarship, see Williams.² Pramukh Swami Maharaj’s own commentary on these texts is preserved in recorded discourses.³

    1. Bhagwan Swaminarayan, Vachanamrut (Ahmedabad: Swaminarayan Aksharpith, 1981), Gadhada I 1.
    2. Raymond Brady Williams, An Introduction to Swaminarayan Hinduism (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 2001), 14–22.
    3. Pramukh Swami Maharaj, “Glory of Bhakti” (discourse, BAPS Mandir, Sarangpur, August 5, 1995).

    Note the two BAPS-specific conventions on shastra citations: (a) shastra titles (Vachanamrut, Bhagavad Gita) are roman, not italicized; (b) no punctuation between title and reference (Gadhada I 1, not Gadhada I.1). See 3.15.7.

    3.15.1 When to Cite¶

    Cite any source you draw on, not just direct quotations.

    Type Definition Cite?
    Quotation Source’s exact words, in quotation marks. ✅ Yes
    Paraphrase Source’s specific ideas restated in your own words. ✅ Yes
    Summary The gist of a source’s argument in your own words. ✅ Yes
    Common knowledge Widely known facts (e.g., dates of Bhagwan Swaminarayan’s lifetime; the existence of the Vachanamrut). ❌ No

    When in doubt, cite.

    3.15.2 The Three Parts of a CMS Citation¶

    A CMS citation has three coordinated components, all of which must agree:

    1. In-text superscript number at the point of reference.
    2. Footnote at the bottom of the page (or endnote at the end of the chapter / book), keyed to the superscript.
    3. Entry in the Bibliography at the end of the publication.

    3.15.2.1 In-Text Superscripts¶

    • Numbers run consecutively from 1 throughout a chapter (or, in shorter pieces, throughout the whole text).
    • Each superscript follows the clause or sentence it references.
    • For placement relative to surrounding punctuation (period, comma, semicolon, colon, dash, quotation mark), see 3.11 Footnotes.

    3.15.2.2 Footnote Conventions¶

    • First citation of a source: full citation (author, title, publication details, page).
    • Subsequent citations: shortened form — author’s last name + 1–4 keywords from the title + page (e.g., Harris, Broadway Theatre, 30.). See 3.15.8.
    • Format: indented first line; un-indented continuation; full numeral (not superscript) followed by a period.

    3.15.2.3 Bibliography Conventions¶

    • One entry per source.
    • Alphabetized by the author’s last name.
    • Hanging indent — first line flush left; subsequent lines indented.
    • Headed Bibliography or Works Cited on its own page at the end of the publication.

    3.15.3 Books¶

    3.15.3.1 Book, Single Author¶

    Footnote format: Firstname Lastname, Title of Book (City: Publisher, Year), page.

    1. Andrew Harris, Broadway Theatre (London: Routledge, 1994), 30.

    Bibliography format: Lastname, Firstname. Title of Book. City: Publisher, Year.

    Harris, Andrew. Broadway Theatre. London: Routledge, 1994.

    3.15.3.2 Book, Two or Three Authors¶

    Footnote format: Firstname1 Lastname1 and Firstname2 Lastname2, Title (City: Publisher, Year), page.

    1. Kitty Locker and Stephen Kaczmarek, Business Communication: Building Critical Skills (New York: McGraw-Hill, 2010), 104.

    Bibliography format: Only the first author is inverted.

    Locker, Kitty, and Stephen Kaczmarek. Business Communication: Building Critical Skills. New York: McGraw-Hill, 2010.

    3.15.3.3 Book, Four or More Authors¶

    Footnote format: Use Lastname et al. in the footnote; list all authors in the bibliography.

    1. John Smith et al., Title of Book (City: Publisher, Year), 50.

    Bibliography format: All authors listed in full.

    Smith, John, Mary Jones, Robert Brown, and Sarah Davis. Title of Book. City: Publisher, Year.

    3.15.3.4 Edited Book / Anthology¶

    Use ed. (singular) or eds. (plural) after the editor name(s).

    Footnote format:

    1. American Theatre Magazine Staff, ed., American Theatre Reader: Essays and Conversations from American Theatre Magazine (New York: Theatre Communications Group, 2009).

    Bibliography format:

    American Theatre Magazine Staff, ed. American Theatre Reader: Essays and Conversations from American Theatre Magazine. New York: Theatre Communications Group, 2009.

    3.15.3.5 Chapter or Essay in an Edited Book¶

    Footnote format: Firstname Lastname of chapter author, “Title of Chapter,” in Title of Book, ed. Firstname Lastname of editor (City: Publisher, Year), page.

    1. Eva Le Gallienne, “On Repertory and Audiences,” in American Theatre Reader: Essays and Conversations from American Theatre Magazine, ed. American Theatre Magazine Staff (New York: Theatre Communications Group, 2009), 29.

    Bibliography format: Include the chapter’s page span (not just the cited page).

    Le Gallienne, Eva. “On Repertory and Audiences.” In American Theatre Reader: Essays and Conversations from American Theatre Magazine, edited by American Theatre Magazine Staff, 29–31. New York: Theatre Communications Group, 2009.

    3.15.3.6 Translated Book¶

    Include the translator after the title.

    Footnote format:

    1. Author Name, Title of Book, trans. Firstname Lastname (City: Publisher, Year), page.

    For BAPS translations from Gujarati, see 3.15.7.12.

    3.15.4 Periodicals¶

    3.15.4.1 Scholarly Journal Article¶

    Footnote format: Firstname Lastname, “Title of Article,” Journal Name volume, no. issue (Year): page, DOI or URL.

    For electronic articles, include a DOI (preferred) or stable URL. Include an access date only if the material is time-sensitive (a frequently-revised web resource).

    1. Srinivas K. Reddy, Vanitha Swaminathan, and Carol M. Motley, “Exploring the Determinants of Broadway Show Success,” Journal of Marketing Research 35, no. 3 (1998): 374, https://doi.org/10.2307/3152034.

    Bibliography format: Include the article’s full page span.

    Reddy, Srinivas K., Vanitha Swaminathan, and Carol M. Motley. “Exploring the Determinants of Broadway Show Success.” Journal of Marketing Research 35, no. 3 (1998): 370–83. https://doi.org/10.2307/3152034.

    3.15.4.2 Magazine Article¶

    Footnote format: Firstname Lastname, “Title of Article,” Magazine Name, Month day, Year [or Month Year], page [if available], URL [if online].

    1. Author Name, “Article Title,” Swaminarayan Bliss, July 2020, 32–37.

    Bibliography format:

    Lastname, Firstname. “Article Title.” Swaminarayan Bliss. July 2020, 32–37.

    3.15.4.3 Newspaper Article¶

    Footnote format: Firstname Lastname, “Title of Article,” Newspaper Name, Month day, Year, URL [if online].

    1. Kaja Whitehouse, “Pedigreed Wall Streeter Accused of Fraud,” USA Today, March 28, 2016, https://www.usatoday.com/story/money/2016/03/28/pedigreed-wall-streeter-accused-fraud/82343104/.

    Bibliography format:

    Whitehouse, Kaja. “Pedigreed Wall Streeter Accused of Fraud.” USA Today. March 28, 2016. https://www.usatoday.com/story/money/2016/03/28/pedigreed-wall-streeter-accused-fraud/82343104/.

    3.15.5 Online Sources¶

    3.15.5.1 Web Page or Online Article¶

    Footnote format: Firstname Lastname [if known], “Title of Page,” Website Publisher, Month day, Year [publication, modification, or access date], URL.

    1. Stan Schroeder, “Facebook Ditches Support for BlackBerry,” Mashable, accessed March 22, 2016, https://mashable.com/2016/03/22/blackberry-facebook-support-end/.

    Bibliography format:

    Schroeder, Stan. “Facebook Ditches Support for BlackBerry.” Mashable. Accessed March 22, 2016. https://mashable.com/2016/03/22/blackberry-facebook-support-end/.

    Use the most specific date available: publication date if known, otherwise modification date, otherwise access date.

    3.15.5.2 Whole Website¶

    Footnote format:

    1. Lewis University Writing Center, accessed September 5, 2024, https://www.lewisu.edu/writingcenter.

    When citing a whole website rather than a specific page, the website’s name takes the role of the title.

    3.15.5.3 Blog Post¶

    Treat as a Web Page (above). Italicize the blog’s name if the blog is a named publication.

    3.15.5.4 Social Media Post¶

    Footnote format: Author or Account Name, post type or first ~160 characters of the post, Platform, Month day, Year, URL.

    CMS treats social-media posts as informal sources; cite them in the footnote, not in the bibliography, unless they are central to the argument.

    3.15.6 Specialized References¶

    3.15.6.1 Encyclopedia or Dictionary Entry¶

    Footnote format: Encyclopedia Name, edition, s.v. “entry term.” For online sources, add access date and URL.

    1. Encyclopedia Britannica, 16th ed., s.v. “style.”
    2. Oxford English Dictionary, 3rd ed., s.v. “style,” accessed September 5, 2024, https://www.oed.com.

    s.v. is Latin for sub verbo (“under the word”) — pointing to the entry alphabetized under that headword.

    Bibliography: Encyclopedia and dictionary entries are not normally included in the bibliography.

    3.15.6.2 Government Document¶

    Footnote format: Issuing Country, Government Division, Subsidiary Divisions [if any], Title of Document, by Firstname Lastname [if available], Report Number (City: Publisher, Year), page [if applicable], URL.

    1. California, Bureau of Investigation, Russian Organized Crime, by Dept. of Justice, Division of Law Enforcement (Sacramento, CA: California, 1996), https://www.fas.org/irp/world/para/docs/rusorg1.htm.

    3.15.6.3 Film or Video¶

    Footnote format: Title of Film, directed by Firstname Lastname (Year of Release; City: Studio, Year of Physical Copy), format, runtime.

    1. Dunkirk, directed by Christopher Nolan (2017; Burbank, CA: Warner Bros., 2017), DVD, 109 min.

    3.15.6.4 Lecture, Discourse, or Unpublished Talk¶

    Footnote format: Firstname Lastname, “Title of Talk” (lecture, Event Name, Location, Month day, Year).

    1. Pramukh Swami Maharaj, “Glory of Bhakti” (discourse, BAPS Mandir, Sarangpur, August 5, 1995).

    For recorded discourses that have been formally published, treat as an audio or video recording (next section).

    3.15.6.5 Audio or Video Recording¶

    Footnote format: Firstname Lastname, Title (City: Publisher, Year), format, runtime, URL [if online].

    3.15.6.6 Personal Communication¶

    Personal communications (interviews, letters, emails) are cited in footnotes only, not in the bibliography.

    1. Sadhu Swayamprakashdas, email message to author, May 13, 2026.

    3.15.6.7 Thesis or Dissertation¶

    Footnote format: Firstname Lastname, “Title of Thesis” (PhD diss. / master’s thesis, University Name, Year), page.

    1. Jane Doe, “Devotional Movements in Nineteenth-Century Gujarat” (PhD diss., University of Oxford, 2018), 102.

    3.15.7 Hindu Shastra Citations¶

    SAP uses a citation convention for Hindu shastras that differs from standard CMS NB style in two specific ways:

    • Shastra titles are roman, capitalized, not italicized (CMS would italicize them as titles of works).
    • No punctuation between the title and the reference numbers — Bhagavad Gita 2.47, not Bhagavad Gita, 2.47 or Bhagavad Gita 2.47.

    The convention balances academic clarity, scriptural authenticity, devotional sensitivity, and readability for a general or youth audience. It applies in prose, captions, footnotes, and bibliography entries throughout SAP publications.

    3.15.7.1 Recognized Shastras (Established English Forms)¶

    Use the established English transliteration of each title — without diacritical marks unless the publication follows a strict academic system (see 5.2 SAP Diacritics Policy). Capitalize as proper nouns; do not italicize.

    BAPS shastras:

    • Vachanamrut
    • Swamini Vato
    • Shikshapatri
    • Satsang Diksha
    • Bhaktachintamani
    • Harililamrut
    • Haricharitramrut Sagar

    Broader Hindu corpus:

    • Bhagavad Gita
    • Mahabharat
    • Ramayan
    • Shrimad Bhagvat
    • Upanishads (Katha Upanishad, Isha Upanishad, Mundaka Upanishad, …)

    Generic categories take lowercase — these are common nouns, not titles:

    • the Hindu scriptures
    • the shastras
    • sacred texts
    • the epics

    3.15.7.2 Citation Format¶

    The general structure for in-text and parenthetical citations is:

    Scripture Name + space + Reference

    with no punctuation between title and reference.

    Examples:

    • Vachanamrut Gadhada I 1
    • Vachanamrut Kariyani 5
    • Bhagavad Gita 2.47
    • Shikshapatri 116
    • Swamini Vato 5.23
    • Katha Upanishad 1.2.18

    3.15.7.3 Vachanamrut¶

    Preferred form:

    Vachanamrut + Section + Discourse Number

    • Roman numerals are retained for the three Gadhada sub-divisions (Gadh. I, Gadh. II, Gadh. III).
    • No punctuation between division and discourse number.

    Examples:

    • Vachanamrut Gadhada I 54
    • Vachanamrut Gadhada II 13
    • Vachanamrut Gadhada III 27
    • Vachanamrut Loya 7
    • Vachanamrut Kariyani 5

    Section abbreviations. In citation-heavy or academic contexts, use the abbreviated forms below; define on first use and use consistently thereafter. Two abbreviation systems are in use — a long form and a short form — pick one per publication.

    Section Long form Short form
    Vachanamrut Vach. V
    Gadhada I Gadh. I G I
    Sarangpur Sar. S
    Kariyani Kar. K
    Loya Lo. L
    Panchala Pan. P
    Gadhada II Gadh. II G II
    Vartal Var. V
    Ahmedabad Amd. A
    Gadhada III Gadh. III G III
    Ashlali Ash. As
    Jetalpur Jet. J
    Bhugol-Khagol Bhu-Kha BK

    Examples in long-abbreviated form: Vach. Gadh. I 54, Vach. Kar. 5, Vach. Loya 7.

    Examples in short-abbreviated form: V G I 54, V K 5, V L 7.

    Footnote format (academic):

    1. Bhagwan Swaminarayan, Vachanamrut (Ahmedabad: Swaminarayan Aksharpith, 1981), Gadhada I 1.

    Bibliography format:

    Bhagwan Swaminarayan. Vachanamrut. Ahmedabad: Swaminarayan Aksharpith, 1981.

    For the open-discussion history of this convention, see 10.4 Shastras.

    3.15.7.4 Swamini Vato¶

    Preferred form:

    Swamini Vato + Chapter (prakaran) + . + Vaat

    Examples:

    • Swamini Vato 1.1
    • Swamini Vato 5.132

    Footnote format:

    1. Aksharbrahma Gunatitanand Swami, Swamini Vato (Ahmedabad: Swaminarayan Aksharpith, 1980), 5.132.

    3.15.7.5 Shikshapatri¶

    Preferred form:

    Shikshapatri + Verse Number

    The verse number alone — no “verse” prefix in the in-text form.

    Examples:

    • Shikshapatri 1
    • Shikshapatri 116

    Footnote format:

    1. Bhagwan Swaminarayan, Shikshapatri (Ahmedabad: Swaminarayan Aksharpith, 1985), 116.

    3.15.7.6 Bhagavad Gita¶

    Preferred form:

    Bhagavad Gita + Chapter.Verse

    Chapter number first, then verse number, separated by a period.

    Examples:

    • Bhagavad Gita 2.47
    • Bhagavad Gita 4.7
    • Bhagavad Gita 18.66

    Avoid: Bhagavad Gita Chapter 4 Verse 7 — verbose and inconsistent with the system used for other shastras.

    Abbreviation (academic or citation-heavy contexts only): BG 4.7. Define on first use.

    3.15.7.7 Upanishads¶

    Use the recognized textual divisions of each Upanishad. Subdivision schemes vary — follow the standard for each.

    Examples:

    • Katha Upanishad 1.2.18
    • Isha Upanishad 1
    • Mundaka Upanishad 3.2.4

    3.15.7.8 Other Hindu Shastras¶

    Apply the same pattern — established English title followed by the recognized textual division:

    • Mahabharat — Mahabharat Shanti Parva 12.45 (parva + chapter); in-text references often drop the parva when context is clear.
    • Ramayan — Ramayan 2.10.5 (kanda.sarga.verse).
    • Shrimad Bhagvat — Shrimad Bhagvat 1.1.1 (canto.chapter.verse).

    3.15.7.9 Use of Abbreviations¶

    Abbreviated forms (Vach. Gadh. I 1, BG 4.7) aid scanning in academic or citation-heavy work — scholarly articles, footnote-dense reference books, comparative studies. They are discouraged in:

    • devotional writing,
    • youth-oriented publications,
    • magazines, biographies, and pastoral material.

    Full forms are preferred for clarity and reverence. When abbreviations are used, define them once on first appearance, and apply consistently throughout the publication.

    3.15.7.10 Quoting Shastras¶

    Short quotations are incorporated directly into the prose with quotation marks. The citation follows immediately, parenthetically or inline:

    As the Bhagavad Gita states, “One who gives up all material desires and lives free from a sense of greed, proprietorship, and egoism, attains perfect peace” (Bhagavad Gita 2.71).

    Long quotations (more than three lines, or any verse-form passage) are formatted as block quotations:

    • separated from the main text,
    • no quotation marks around the block,
    • citation placed immediately after the quotation, prefixed with a spaced en dash.

    Whenever dharma declines and adharma rises, I manifest myself to protect the righteous and destroy the wicked, and to establish dharma again. – Bhagavad Gita 4.7–4.8

    For the typographic treatment of transliterated verse text within a block (italics, Sanskrit text, macron-only diacritics), see 3.6.4 Shlokas, Padas, and Other Quoted Verses.

    3.15.7.11 Placement of Shastra Citations¶

    Inline citations — the reference is integrated into the prose:

    Bhagwan Swaminarayan explains in Vachanamrut Gadhada I 54 that true association shapes one’s inner inclinations.

    Bhagwan Swaminarayan explains in Vachanamrut Kariyani 5 that desires motivated by worldly enjoyment bind the jiva.

    Parenthetical citations — the reference is at the end of the supporting clause, in parentheses:

    True spiritual progress depends upon association with the Satpurush (Vachanamrut Gadhada I 54).

    The Bhagavad Gita teaches disciplined action without attachment to results (Bhagavad Gita 2.47).

    Quotation attribution — verse or scripture quoted as a block, with the reference following on its own line, prefixed by a spaced en dash:

    “One who gives up all material desires and lives free from a sense of greed, proprietorship, and egoism, attains perfect peace.” – Bhagavad Gita 2.71

    3.15.7.12 Translation Note¶

    When citing a published English translation of a Gujarati or Sanskrit shastra, indicate the translator in the footnote form (the in-text shastra citation stays the same):

    In-text: Vachanamrut Gadhada I 1 Footnote: 22. Bhagwan Swaminarayan, Vachanamrut, trans. Sadhu Swayamprakashdas (Ahmedabad: Swaminarayan Aksharpith, 2014), Gadhada I 1.

    3.15.7.13 Recorded Discourses¶

    For published recordings of Pramukh Swami Maharaj’s or Mahant Swami Maharaj’s discourses (the katha series, commemorative volumes, etc.), treat as Audio/Video Recording (3.15.6.5).

    For unpublished or internal Sanstha recordings (private discourses, archive material), treat as Personal Communication (3.15.6.6).

    3.15.8 Ibid. and Shortened-Form Citations¶

    3.15.8.1 Ibid. (Immediate Repeat)¶

    When the next footnote cites the same source as the immediately preceding one, use Ibid. (italicized, with period) + page:

    1. Andrew Harris, Broadway Theatre (London: Routledge, 1994), 30.
    2. Ibid., 32.
    3. Ibid.

    Use Ibid. alone (no page) when the page is also the same.

    3.15.8.2 Shortened Form (Non-Immediate Repeat)¶

    When citing a source again after intervening notes from other sources, use the shortened form: author’s last name + 1–4 keywords from the title + page:

    1. Andrew Harris, Broadway Theatre (London: Routledge, 1994), 30.
    2. ...some other source...
    3. Harris, Broadway Theatre, 45.

    3.15.8.3 Op. cit. and Loc. cit.¶

    Don’t use these. CMS deprecated op. cit. and loc. cit. — use the shortened form (3.15.8.2) instead.

    3.15.9 Common Pitfalls¶

    • ❌ Missing publication city. Always include the city of publication for books.
    • ❌ Inconsistent author-name order. Footnotes always use Firstname Lastname; bibliography always uses Lastname, Firstname (first author only).
    • ❌ Italics on the title of an article or chapter. Article and chapter titles take quotation marks, not italics. Italics are reserved for whole works — book titles, journal names, film titles. Exception: Hindu shastra titles (Bhagavad Gita, Vachanamrut, Shikshapatri, etc.) are roman, not italicized — a BAPS-specific departure from CMS. See 3.15.7.
    • ❌ Missing DOI for an online article. Always prefer the DOI over the raw URL when both are available.
    • ❌ Putting personal communications in the bibliography. Personal communications are cited in footnotes only (3.15.6.6).
    • ❌ Using op. cit. or loc. cit. Deprecated; use the shortened form (3.15.8.2).
    • ❌ Mixing styles within a single publication. CMS NB consistently throughout, or not at all. If a co-author drafts in APA, convert.

    For broader editorial pitfalls (Indian-English usages, Gujarati calques, devotional register), see Part 11 Common Editorial Pitfalls.
