# 36 Base Style Manual
Open question on whether to adopt a professionally published style manual as the base for SAP. Reproduced from the **Conventions to Discuss** section of the SAP Writing Guidelines (v1).

> Should we adopt a professionally published style manual as our base? E.g., Chicago Manual of Style, Associated Press Manual, Oxford,…
>
> When necessary and can be correctly done, if we prefer to tweak the chosen style guide's convention, we can add that scenario to our in-house guide.

## 36.1 What "Adopt a Base" Would Mean
A base style manual gives us a default ruling for any question this guide does not cover. Today, when an author asks "comma before *too* at the end of a sentence?" or "ellipsis with bracketed gloss?" or "hyphen in *brand-new*?", there's no shared answer to point to.

Adopting (for example) the *Chicago Manual of Style* would mean: "If this guide is silent, follow Chicago. If we want to depart from Chicago, we document the departure in this guide."

## 36.2 Candidates
- **Chicago Manual of Style (CMOS)** — the dominant US scholarly/trade reference. Strong on citation, quotations, and Indian/Sanskrit conventions (Chicago has a long-standing transliteration appendix).
- **Associated Press Stylebook (AP)** — US journalism standard. Compact, sentence-case-friendly, strong on names and titles. Less suited to long-form scholarly material.
- **New Hart's Rules** (Oxford) — the British counterpart to CMOS. Strong on British spelling, hyphenation, and capitalization of titles. A natural fit if we go BrE-default.
- **The Economist Style Guide** — clean, opinionated, BrE-leaning, journalistic. Less detailed than the others but pleasant to read.

## 36.3 Considerations
- **BrE vs AmE** — see the related question in [Web — BrE or AmE?](web.md). Choosing CMOS pulls AmE; choosing Hart's pulls BrE.
- **Indic conventions** — CMOS and Hart's both have appendices on Indic transliteration; AP does not.
- **Cost / accessibility** — only authors and editors with the actual book on their shelf can quickly look up an answer. CMOS Online subscription works around this.
- **Scope of departures** — every departure adds maintenance cost. The fewer the better.
