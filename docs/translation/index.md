# 2.1 Translation & Transliteration

*Last reviewed by the SAP DTP team: 2026-05-13.*

Rules for translating from Gujarati, Sanskrit, or Hindi into English – and for deciding what to translate, what to transliterate, and what to leave anglicized.

Most SAP English work is translated from Gujarati source texts. Sanskrit material – typically scriptural verses and commentaries – is usually embedded in larger Gujarati→English projects; Hindi material is similarly embedded. A significant proportion of work is also written originally in English.

For the underlying definitions, see [Foundational Concepts](../concepts/index.md).

## 2.1.2 At a Glance
| Word type | Form | Italics? | Diacritics? |
|---|---|---|---|
| In the OED (anglicized) | OED spelling | No | No |
| Personal/place name | SAP transliteration, no diacritics | No | No |
| Title of a work | Use the official English title if one exists; otherwise transliterate | Italics for books/periodicals | No (in titles) |
| Other Indic term | SAP transliteration | Yes (italics) | Per project (verses may use diacritics; see [Diacritics](../diacritics/index.md)) |

For the formatting consequences, see [Editorial – Italics](../editorial/italics.md), [Plurals](../editorial/plurals.md), and [Quotation Marks](../editorial/quotation-marks.md).
