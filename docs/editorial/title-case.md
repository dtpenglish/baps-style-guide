# 3.4 Title Case in Detail

*Last reviewed by the SAP DTP team: 2026-05-13.*

The basic rule for titles of works lives at [3.3.1.3](capitalization.md#3313-titles-of-works). The sections below cover the specific cases that recur in book titles, chapter labels, and the headings on this site.

## 3.4.1 What to Capitalize, What to Lowercase
**Capitalize:**

- nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs;
- subordinating conjunctions (*as*, *because*, *that*, *if*, *unless*, *until*, *whenever*);
- the **first** and **last** word of the title, regardless of part of speech.

**Lowercase:**

- articles (*a*, *an*, *the*);
- coordinating conjunctions (*and*, *but*, *or*, *nor*, *for*, *yet*, *so*);
- prepositions of any length (*at*, *by*, *for*, *from*, *in*, *of*, *on*, *to*, *with*, *between*, *through*, *across*, *about*);
- the infinitive *to* (*How to Read*, not *How To Read*).

> *The Vachanamrut: A Reader's Guide to the Discourses of Bhagwan Swaminarayan*

This follows CMS, which treats word *class* as the deciding factor — not word length. (AP capitalizes prepositions of four or more letters; SAP does not.)

## 3.4.2 Hyphenated Words in Titles
- **Capitalize the first element** always.
- **Capitalize subsequent elements** unless they are articles, prepositions, or coordinating conjunctions.
- **Capitalize the second element of a spelled-out compound number**: *Forty-Ninth Street Blues*.
- **Don't capitalize the second element if the first is a prefix** that can't stand alone (*anti-*, *pre-*, *de-*, *non-*): *Anti-inflammatory Dieting*, *Pre-modern Era*, *Non-violent Resistance*.

| Form | Example |
|---|---|
| Both elements substantive | *High-Quality Web Services* |
| Hyphenated compound number | *Forty-Ninth Street Blues* |
| Conjunction or article in middle | *Bed-and-Breakfast Options* |
| Article or preposition in middle | *Up-to-Date Catalogue* |
| Prefix that can't stand alone | *Anti-inflammatory Dieting* |
| Two-element ordinal compound | *Twenty-First-Century Outlook* |

## 3.4.3 First Word after a Colon
Capitalize the first word after a colon when what follows is itself a complete clause (and the surrounding form is title case):

> *Feminine Poetry: Ten Women Writers from Around the World*

In **sentence case**, capitalize after a colon only if the clause is independent and stands alone:

> I know who you are: You are my friend.

If the post-colon material is a fragment that can't stand alone, lowercase it:

> I know who you are: nobody.

## 3.4.4 Phrasal Verbs and Open Compounds in Titles
Two title-only quirks for multi-word patterns. Both apply only when setting a title or subhead in title case, not in body prose.

**Phrasal verbs** — capitalize the preposition when it forms part of a phrasal verb (the preposition is functioning as part of the verb, not introducing a prepositional phrase):

- *How to **Back Up** a Computer* (*back up* = make a copy)
- *Turn **Down** the Heat to Save You Money* (*turn down* = reduce)
- *Look **Up** the Reference* (*look up* = consult)

The same words stay lowercase when used in their ordinary prepositional sense:

- *Notes **on** the Verb*
- *Reflections **from** the Road*

**Open compounds** — when a modifier and a noun together form a recognized open compound (effectively a noun phrase), capitalize both elements:

- *Salad Dressing Recipes*
- *The Best Science Fiction and Fantasy of the Year*
- *Master Class Notes*

## 3.4.5 Sentence Case vs. Title Case — When to Use Which
- **Title case** — capitalize all principal words per the rules above — for headings on this site, digital publications, book titles, chapter titles, magazine and newspaper titles. This is the default everywhere except definitions.
- **Sentence case** — first word and proper nouns only — reserved for **definitions** in glossaries and term-and-meaning tables. See [3.3.2.16](capitalization.md#33216-definitions-use-sentence-case-not-title-case).

A glossary entry illustrates the distinction: the term itself uses title case (*Param Ekantik Sant*), while its definition uses sentence case (*Ideal sadhu of God, referring to the Satpurush.*).
