# Plurals

How to form the plural of an Indic word in SAP English publications. The rules below are reproduced from the SAP Writing Guidelines (v1).

## The rules

### Plurals of transliterated Indic words

Plurals of Indic words written in roman follow their **native plurals**:

- *rotlo* → *rotla*

Or form the plural of a transliterated Indic word by adding a roman ***s*** to the italic word:

- *rotlo* → *rotlo*s

For **anglicised** words, follow the spelling in the OED:

- chapatis / chapattis

### Avoid anglicised plurals on fully transliterated words

Avoid anglicised plurals on **fully transliterated plural** Indic words. Use the native plural form, not an English ‑*s*:

- ✅ *rotla*
- ❌ *rotlas* / *rotlos*

Anglicised plurals **may** be used on words that are found in the OED (and thus are not fully transliterated):

- pandits
- gurus

## Decision summary

| Source word | Form in OED? | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| chapati | Yes | chapatis (per OED) |
| pandit | Yes | pandits |
| *rotlo* | No | *rotla* (native plural) — or *rotlo*s with roman *s* |
| *kathiyavadi* | No | *kathiyavadi*s (roman *s*) |

## Open question — singular Gujarati words used as their own plural

> How should we use singular Gujarati words that are their own plural? E.g., *dandvat*, *pradakshina*, *pranam*… — even though in English we (SAP) do add an *s* in plural usage.

This is an unresolved question in the SAP Writing Guidelines. See [Open Discussions: Indic Words](../discussions/indic-words.md) for the running discussion.
