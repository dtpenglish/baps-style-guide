    4 Tithis¶

    How to write the names of the days of the lunar fortnight in BAPS English publications.

    4.1 The Rule¶

    Tithis are capitalized, set in roman, not italicized, and carry no diacritics — in every position they appear.

    This applies whether the tithi name appears alone, in a date, or as part of a compound festival name.

    Use Form
    Standalone He observes a fast on every Ekadashi.
    In a date The murti was installed on Magshar sud Ekadashi, V.S. 2080.
    In a compound name Sharad Punam, Devshayani Ekadashi, Chaitra sud Padvo.
    Paired with the fortnight marker The fortnight designators sud (waxing) and vad (waning) are italic, lowercase: Bhadarvo vad Bij.

    4.2 The 16 Tithis¶

    The Indian lunar month has 30 tithis — 15 in the waxing fortnight (sud / shukla paksha) and 15 in the waning fortnight (vad / krishna paksha). The 15th of the waning fortnight is Amas (new moon); the 15th of the waxing fortnight is Punam (full moon). The other 14 names repeat across both fortnights.

    # Gujarati form Sanskrit form Notes
    1 Padvo Pratipada First day of the fortnight
    2 Bij Dwitiya
    3 Trij Tritiya
    4 Choth Chaturthi
    5 Pancham Panchami
    6 Chhath Shashthi
    7 Satam Saptami
    8 Atham Ashtami
    9 Nom Navami
    10 Dasham Dashami
    11 Ekadashi Ekadashi Fasting day; recurs twice each lunar month
    12 Baras Dwadashi
    13 Teras Trayodashi
    14 Chaudash Chaturdashi
    15 (waxing) Punam Purnima Full moon
    15 (waning) Amas Amavasya New moon

    Use the form (Gujarati or Sanskrit) appropriate to the publication’s source language. In Gujarati-source BAPS work the forms in the middle column are standard; in Sanskrit-quoting passages the right-hand column may appear.

    4.3 Examples in Context¶

    Each example shows the rule applied in a different sentence shape:

    • BAPS Mahant Swami Maharaj will perform the abhishek on Sharad Punam.
    • Devotees fast on every Ekadashi, taking one meal of fruit and milk after sunset.
    • The shilanyas was performed on Magshar sud Padvo, V.S. 2080 (28 November 2023).
    • Devshayani Ekadashi marks the start of Chaturmas; Devuthi Ekadashi marks its end.
    • The festival fell on Bhadarvo vad Atham — Janmashtami.
    • Between Punam and Amas lies the dark fortnight of Shravan.
    • The patotsav is held annually on Maha sud Bij.

    4.4 Why This Is the Rule¶

    Three Chicago Manual of Style (CMS) clauses converge on the same answer; Hart’s Rules (the BrE counterpart) reaches it identically.

    4.4.1 CMS 8.79 — Days of the Week and Months¶

    “The days of the week and the months of the year are capitalized.”

    Padvo, Bij, Trij… are the direct functional analogue of Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday — they are the named days of the lunar fortnight. Tuesday doesn’t drop its capital when used generically (every Tuesday); neither should Ekadashi.

    4.4.2 CMS 8.92 — Religious Holidays, Fasts, and Seasons¶

    “The names of religious holidays, fasts, and seasons are usually capitalized.”

    CMS lists Diwali, Ramadan, Yom Kippur, Lent among its examples. Tithis belong to that set: Ekadashi and Punam are explicitly fast days and observance days within the Vaishnava liturgical calendar.

    4.4.3 CMS 7.55 / 11.3 — Foreign Words Integrated into English¶

    “Words and phrases now adopted into English need not be italicized, especially if a definition appears nearby or the term is widely known.”

    In a BAPS or Swaminarayan publication aimed at the satsangi readership for which SAP publishes, tithi names are as familiar as Diwali or Easter. They cross the threshold from “italicized foreign term” to “roman established designation” — so no italics.

    4.4.4 CMS — Documented Religious-Context Allowance¶

    While CMS strict practice would lowercase tithi names (treating them as ordinal dates), it explicitly permits capitalizing tithi names in religious or devotional contexts as a style choice to reflect reverence — provided the choice is applied consistently. SAP publications take that documented option.

    4.4.5 Hart’s Rules / New Oxford Style Manual (BrE)¶

    Hart’s reaches the same conclusion via the same logic:

    • Religious feast days, fasts, and named days are capitalized (Hart’s §5.1.6).
    • Loanwords that have been “naturalized” into the publication’s English drop italics (Hart’s §7.1).

    There is no BrE/AmE divergence on this point — both varieties capitalize.

    4.4.6 Precedent in the OED¶

    The Oxford English Dictionary enters Hindu festival and observance names as proper nouns: Diwali (capitalized), Holi (capitalized), Navaratri (capitalized). Treating Ekadashi and Punam the same way is consistent with how the OED handles their nearest analogues.

    4.4.7 Precedent in Wikipedia, Britannica, and Penguin India House Style¶

    All three sources, in their English-language editorial style, capitalize tithi names (Ekadashi, Purnima, Amavasya) and set them in roman without italics. None lowercase or italicize.

    4.5 What This Rule Does not Override¶

    The general SAP rule — “Indic words not in the OED are italicized” — still holds for the broader category of religious common nouns. Tithis are an explicit exception because they function as proper-noun calendar names. Compare:

    Word type Form Example
    Day of the week (English) Roman, Cap every Tuesday
    Month (English) Roman, Cap in March
    Religious holiday in OED Roman, Cap Diwali, Easter
    Tithi (this rule) Roman, Cap, no italics Ekadashi, Punam, Padvo
    Indic festival not in OED Roman, Cap Janmashtami, Annakut, Pushpadolotsav
    Generic religious act Italic, lowercase darshan, arti, katha
    Generic religious role Italic, lowercase sadhu, kothari, mahant

    Tithis pattern with the named days (rows 1, 2, 3, 4) — not with the generic religious acts (row 6).

    4.6 Edge Cases¶

    4.6.1 Tithi Itself, as a Meta-Term¶

    The word tithi — meaning “a lunar day” in the abstract — is a generic common noun, not the name of any specific day. It follows the standard rule for non-anglicized Indic terms: italic, lowercase.

    Today’s tithi is Maha sud Pancham.

    (tithi in italic lowercase; the month Maha and the tithi Pancham in roman caps; sud in italic lowercase — see below.)

    4.6.2 Fortnight Designators — sud and vad¶

    The fortnight designators sud (waxing fortnight, shukla paksha) and vad (waning fortnight, krishna paksha) are italic and lowercase, every time — when paired with a tithi in a date and when discussed in the abstract:

    • Magshar sud Padvo
    • Bhadarvo vad Bij
    • The vad fortnight begins after Punam.
    • During the sud of Shravan, devotees observe…

    This treatment differs from the tithis themselves (which are roman, capitalized) because sud and vad function as descriptors of which half of the lunar month a tithi falls in, not as named days in their own right. They are SAP house style for fortnight designators across all publications.

    The Sanskrit equivalents shukla paksha and krishna paksha follow the same form — italic, lowercase — when used in their generic sense.

    4.6.3 Variant Spellings¶

    A handful of tithi names have variant English spellings in current circulation. The forms above are the SAP-preferred spellings:

    Preferred Sometimes seen
    Bij Beej
    Choth Chouth, Chauth
    Chhath Chhat, Chath
    Atham Aatham, Aatam
    Punam Poonam
    Amas Amaas, Amavas

    For unsettled variants, see the live discussion in Open Discussions: Transliteration.

    4.6.4 Sanskrit Forms in English Text¶

    Sanskrit forms (Pratipada, Dwitiya, Purnima, etc.) follow the same rule — roman, capitalized, no italics, no diacritics — when referring to the named day:

    The verse is recited on Purnima.

    The Sanskrit form is appropriate where the source text uses it, or where the readership is more familiar with Sanskrit calendar terms. Within a single publication, be consistent — don’t mix Punam and Purnima arbitrarily.

    4.6.5 When a Tithi Appears inside an Italicized Phrase¶

    If an entire phrase is italicized (e.g. a Sanskrit or Gujarati line being quoted), the tithi name participates in that italicization and is set italic too — same as any other word would be:

    The verse begins, “māgha-māsi sitā-pakṣe pratipadī…”

    This is no different from how the word Tuesday would be italicized inside an italicized English phrase.

    4.6.6 The 12 Gujarati Months¶

    Month names follow the same form as tithis: roman, capitalized, no italics, no diacritics — in every position. Treat them like English month names (March, April).

    The 12 months in calendar order, beginning with Kartak (the month following Diwali):

    # Gujarati form Sanskrit form Notes
    1 Kartak Karttika Gujarati New Year (sud Padvo)
    2 Magshar Margashirsha
    3 Posh Pausha
    4 Maha Magha
    5 Fagan Phalguna Holi (sud Punam)
    6 Chaitra Chaitra Vikram Samvat New Year; Rama Navmi (sud Nom)
    7 Vaishakh Vaishakha
    8 Jeth Jyeshtha
    9 Ashadh Ashadha Chaturmas begins — Devshayani Ekadashi (sud Ekadashi)
    10 Shravan Shravana Janmashtami (vad Atham)
    11 Bhadarvo Bhadrapada
    12 Aso Ashvina Navratri, Dussehra; Diwali (vad Amas)

    Use the form (Gujarati or Sanskrit) appropriate to the publication’s source language. In Gujarati-source BAPS work the forms in the middle column are standard; in Sanskrit-quoting passages the right-hand column may appear. Within a single publication, be consistent — don’t mix Magshar and Margashirsha arbitrarily.

    Used with a fortnight designator and a tithi to form a date:

    • Magshar sud Padvo
    • Bhadarvo vad Atham
    • Maha sud Pancham
    • Kartak sud Punam — Dev Diwali
    • Aso vad Amas — Diwali

    In compound names with a tithi, the month behaves as a proper-noun modifier and stays cap roman — e.g. Magshar Punam (the full moon of Magshar), Vaishakh Punam.

    4.7 Open Variants Superseded by This Rule¶

    This rule resolves a previously open question about tithi capitalization and italicization:

    Capitalization/italicization of tithis: Padvo / padvo, Ekadashi / ekadashi, Punam / punam.

    The decision: always capitalized, always roman, never italicized. The lowercase-italic forms (padvo, ekadashi, punam) are not acceptable for tithi names in finished publications.
